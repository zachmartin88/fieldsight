Where the data comes from

Live

This season's satellite crop map (Aug 2026, 10 m). Built from Sentinel-2 and Landsat imagery taken during this growing season and released a few days after each month ends. About 98% accurate for major crops by August. When a field reads clearly, it's marked Live.

Was …

The live map shows a different crop than this field has grown for years, for example vineyards where there were almond orchards. The field may have been replanted, or the satellite may have misread it.

Mixed

More than one thing in view: a field edge, a farmstead, or two crops side by side.

USDA 2025

USDA NASS Cropland Data Layer, the official annual crop map, released each February for the season before. It's used where the live map has a gap, together with the last five years to predict this year's crop from the rotation.

Fields

USDA Crop Sequence Boundaries, the official field outlines with each field's crop history, when you zoom in close. Where those aren't available, field shapes are traced from the crop map.

Parcels

Property lines and owners come from states that publish their records free (WI, NC, AR, FL, CO, VT, CT, plus parcel lines for OH, IN, ND, CA, UT, NJ). Shown only when you tap for them.

Maps are served by George Mason University's CSISS from USDA NASS data. Crop maps are made of 10–30 m squares, so field edges can be off by one square. While driving, each side is read from a strip 30–130 m off the road, so ditches and shoulders don't count.
